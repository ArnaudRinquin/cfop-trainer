CFOP is the world's most popular speedsolving method — the one every sub-15 solver uses. The name spells out the four steps in order: **C**ross, **F**2L, **O**LL, **P**LL. You take a scrambled cube and pass it through four checkpoints. That's it.

- **Cross** — solve the four edges of one face (the bottom). Intuitive, no algorithms. ~7 moves.
- **F2L** — _First Two Layers_. Pair a corner with its edge, slot the pair into place. Repeat 4 times. Intuitive, then algorithmic. ~30 moves.
- **OLL** — _Orient Last Layer_. Make the top face one solid color. One algorithm from 57. ~10 moves.
- **PLL** — _Permute Last Layer_. Move the top pieces to their correct spots. One algorithm from 21. ~12 moves.

A full CFOP solve takes ~55–60 moves. World-class is sub-7 seconds. Sub-20 is a realistic goal for most learners.

### Why not just solve layer by layer?

The beginner method ("layer by layer", LBL) solves layer 1, then layer 2, then layer 3. F2L is the breakthrough: it solves layers 1 and 2 _at the same time_ by inserting corner-edge pairs together. Same number of pieces, half the steps, way faster execution.

CFOP also separates the last layer into _orient_ then _permute_. Each phase has a finite, learnable set of cases. You're never improvising at the end.

<div class="tip">
  You don't need to learn all 57 OLLs + 21 PLLs to start. The "2-Look" stages teach 10 OLLs + 6 PLLs that solve every case in two algorithms instead of one. Plenty fast to break sub-25.
</div>
